Tourist segment clustering

What the clusters mean
Method: both features are standardized (z-score), then k-means (k=2, Euclidean distance) assigns each state to the nearer of two centroids, refit for a few iterations until stable — the same algorithm used for larger customer/region segmentation problems, just run here on a very small, honestly-disclosed sample.
Limitation: n=4 states is too small to generalize from. Perak (fastest-growing) and Sarawak (secondary source) are excluded here because they lack a matching pair of figures — re-run once DOSM publishes the full 16-state table.

Methodology

How this forecast is built, and its limits

Two models, kept separate on purpose. (1) A standard least-squares linear regression is fitted only to 2006–2019 — Tourism Malaysia's own clean, single-methodology series before COVID-19 border closures — giving a real long-run baseline growth rate. (2) Only two fully DOSM-confirmed, methodology-matched post-pandemic points exist so far (2023 and 2024, both from the same "Statistics in Brief 2024" release); 2025 adds a third real data point — 42.2M arrivals, +11.2% YoY — announced by the Ministry of Tourism in January 2026, ahead of DOSM's own "Statistics in Brief 2025" release, so it is shown as preliminary rather than final. With this few confirmed/preliminary points, a full regression on the recovery period alone would still be overfit. Instead, the 2026 forecast applies the same simple, disclosed heuristic as before: continue the latest confirmed/preliminary 2024→2025 growth rate at half its magnitude — a standard, conservative way to model a post-shock recovery curve tapering toward normal growth, rather than assuming the reopening surge repeats at full strength.

Known limitation: Tourism Malaysia's earlier KPI 2023 report shows a provisional 2023 figure (20.14M) that was later revised upward to 28.96M in the final Statistics in Brief 2024 release — we use the latter as the authoritative figure and flag the discrepancy here rather than silently picking one. The same caution applies to 2025: 42.2M is a ministerial announcement (cross-checked across multiple independent news reports), not yet DOSM's own finalized "Statistics in Brief 2025" release — if that release lands on a different number, as happened with 2023, this dashboard should be updated to the finalized figure and the 2026 forecast recomputed from it. As more confirmed years accumulate post-pandemic, this should be replaced with a proper regression or time-series model (e.g. ARIMA / Prophet) fitted to the recovery period alone.

Methodology

How this is calculated, and what it deliberately excludes

Two calculation types, chosen by what each state actually publishes. Type A — visitor scaling (Selangor, WP Kuala Lumpur): both a visitor count and a receipts figure are published for 2024, so we compute this state's real average spend per visitor (receipts ÷ visitors) and hold that ratio constant while scaling visitors by your chosen %. Type B — direct receipts scaling (Pahang, Sabah): no visitor count is published for these states, only receipts and a 2023 comparison, so the % change is applied directly to the 2024 receipts figure instead — a coarser but still fully real-data-based estimate.

Deliberately excluded: Perak (has visitor/tourist figures but no receipts figure, so no spend ratio exists), Sarawak (its receipts are flagged secondary/unconfirmed on Data & Methodology), and every other state (no receipts published at all). We also do not offer a "build N hotel rooms" or capacity-based scenario, despite it being an intuitive policy lever, because no accommodation-capacity dataset is part of this project — adding one would mean inventing a number, which this dashboard does not do.

Who this is for

Purpose & audience

This dashboard is built primarily for government policymakers and planning agencies — tourism ministries, state economic planning units, and statistical bodies — to support tourism policy planning, resource allocation and infrastructure decisions with evidence rather than anecdote. Tourism-industry stakeholders (hotels, attractions, travel agents, transport operators) are a secondary audience who can use the same regional and economic signals to refine their own operations.

Methodology

Where AI/ML is actually used here

Executive Overview insights are rule-based, not a trained model: each one compares official figures against prior-year values or simple thresholds (highest YoY growth, top-N by receipts, count of states missing a published breakdown). Intentional for v1 — every insight traces to one number in one official release, no black box.

Demand Forecast uses a real least-squares linear regression, fitted live in your browser to Tourism Malaysia's 2006–2019 arrivals series, plus a disclosed heuristic (half the confirmed/preliminary 2024→2025 growth rate) for the 2026 near-term projection — full reasoning on that tab.

Tourist segment clustering (Regional Intelligence tab) runs a real k-means (k=2) over the 4 states with matched receipts + growth figures — a genuine unsupervised-learning pass, on a small, honestly-disclosed sample. The Policy Simulator tab is not a model at all — it's disclosed, transparent arithmetic (visitor scaling or direct receipts scaling) on the same real 2024 figures, held out separately so it's never mistaken for a prediction.

Rather than force a speculative correlation onto real numbers, we left both out — consistent with the same rule applied everywhere else on this dashboard: no figure appears unless it genuinely measures tourism, and no relationship is implied unless the source data actually supports it.
Data discipline

Ground rules we followed

Official sources first (Portal Rasmi DOSM, OpenDOSM, data.gov.my, Tourism Malaysia/MOTAC) — every figure above is dated, cited, and traceable to the specific portal it came from. Definitions kept separate: tourist vs excursionist vs visitor are never blended, and inbound figures are never summed with GDP-contribution figures. Where a state has no officially published individual breakdown, it is labelled "data pending" rather than estimated or interpolated — we chose incompleteness over a fabricated number.
